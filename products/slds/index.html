SLDs and LEDs

Superluminescent diodes (SLDs) are broadband incoherent light sources that combine features of both laser diodes (LDs) and light-emitting diodes (LEDs), providing high optical output power with low temporal coherence. It is important to distinguish between light-emitting diodes (LEDs), which emit light from a large surface of the chip, and superluminescent diodes (SLDs), which emit light from the edge of the chip, similar to laser diodes.

We offer SLDs at 830 nm and 980 nm with an optical power of 2 mW and at 1310 nm and 1550 nm with an optical power of 1 mW, as well as LEDs at 650 nm and 850 nm coupled to multimode fiber. All of them are available in coaxial packages without thermal stabilization. The 1310 nm and 1550 nm SLDs and the LEDs are also available in butterfly (BTF) packages with a thermoelectric cooler (TEC) for active temperature control.

Surface-emitting light-emitting diodes (LEDs) are incoherent sources of optical radiation. Unlike superluminescent diodes (SLDs), which emit light from the edge of the chip, LEDs emit light from the surface. This structural difference makes LED technology significantly simpler and more cost-effective. Fiber-coupled LEDs are used as light sources for testing fiber optics and in fiber-optic sensors.

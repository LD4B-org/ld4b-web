Company

What is LD4B?

LD4B is a photonics company specializing in fiber-coupling of semiconductor laser diodes, superluminescent diodes (SLDs), and photodiodes. We design and manufacture advanced fiber-coupled laser and detector modules that deliver high performance and reliability.

Where are LD4B products used?

LD4B products are widely used in microwave photonics, optical time-domain reflectometry, telecommunications, datacom, fiber sensing, medical equipment, and scientific research.

What does LD4B mean?

LD4B stands for “Laser Diode for Business.” The name reflects our focus on providing high-quality optoelectronic solutions. While our roots are in laser diodes, we also manufacture superluminescent diodes (SLDs) and photodiodes, all with advanced fiber-coupling technology.

Who works at LD4B?

LD4B is driven by a team of engineers and scientists passionate about photonics. Our expertise covers precise optical fiber alignment and the manufacturing of reliable fiber-coupled laser diodes, superluminescent diodes (SLDs), and photodiodes.

Where is LD4B located?

LD4B is located in Warsaw, Poland. Our facility at ul. Konstruktorska 6, lokal 108, 02-673 Warsaw manufactures fiber-coupled laser diodes, superluminescent diodes (SLDs), and photodiodes for customers worldwide.

Is LD4B a manufacturer?

Yes. LD4B manufactures fiber-coupled modules, but we do not grow wafers or make semiconductor chips. We source laser diodes, SLDs and photodiodes in TO-cans from chip manufacturers and specialize in what happens next: fiber coupling, active alignment, packaging and testing, carried out at our facility in Warsaw, Poland.

Choosing a module

How do I choose the right LD4B light source or photodetector for my application?

Start with the wavelength, spectrum, power and speed your application needs. Choose a DFB laser for a single-frequency, narrow-linewidth source (telecom, CWDM, sensing, spectroscopy), an FP laser when you simply need power at a given wavelength (500 to 1650 nm, up to 100 mW from the fiber), an SLD for broadband, low-coherence light (OCT, fiber sensing), and a TEC-cooled version (the coaxTEC series or BTF) when wavelength stability matters. For detection, a PIN photodiode covers monitoring and high-speed analog links up to 20 GHz, an APD gives extra sensitivity for weak signals such as in LIDAR and OTDR, and our PIN-TIA and APD-TIA receivers are ready-made solutions for digital links up to 25 Gbps. Then pick the fiber (SM, PM or MM), the connector and the package: every module is built to your configuration. If you're not sure, send your requirements to contact@ld4b.com and we'll recommend the right part.

What technical information should I send to LD4B to get a product recommendation or a quote?

Please send your application, wavelength, optical power, modulation speed, operating temperature, fiber type, connector, package and quantity. If you're unsure about any of these, just describe your application and we'll help you choose.

Do I need a laser diode driver, and how do I choose one?

Yes, every laser diode needs a stable current source. Most of our customers design their own driver boards for their specific device, but any standard laser diode driver works if its current range and modulation speed suit the laser, with a TEC controller added for cooled modules. We also offer controllers from trusted partners that can be ordered together with our modules, and we're happy to help you choose.

Laser Diodes

What are fiber-coupled laser diodes?

Fiber-coupled laser diodes are semiconductor lasers that emit coherent light directly into an optical fiber. They simplify the integration of lasers into optoelectronic systems, providing precise, stable, and efficient light delivery for applications in telecommunications, sensing, and industrial laser systems.

What laser diodes does LD4B offer?

LD4B offers fiber-coupled laser diodes with wavelengths from 500 nm to 1654 nm. Most of our diodes are coupled to single-mode or polarization-maintaining fibers, delivering tens of milliwatts of optical power from the fiber for high-precision applications in industrial photonics.

Does LD4B offer telecom laser diodes?

Yes. LD4B offers fiber-coupled Fabry-Perot and DFB laser diodes for the telecom range from 1270 nm to 1654 nm, including CWDM wavelengths, with modulation speeds up to 10 Gbps and up to 50 mW of optical power from the fiber. They come in coaxial packages, with or without a mounting bracket, and with a TEC and thermistor either in the coaxial COAXB package (the coaxTEC series) or in the 14-pin butterfly (BTF) package, coupled to single-mode or polarization-maintaining fiber.

What is the difference between Fabry-Perot and DFB laser diodes?

A DFB (Distributed Feedback) laser diode is a single-frequency laser with a narrow linewidth and a single spectral peak. A Fabry-Perot (FP) laser diode has no frequency-selective element, producing a wider spectrum with multiple modes.

What is the linewidth of a DFB laser diode?

The linewidth of a DFB laser diode is typically less than 1 MHz, making it too narrow to measure with standard grating-based optical spectrum analyzers. Specialized techniques, such as the self-delayed heterodyne method, are required for accurate linewidth measurement.

What is the difference between single-mode and multimode laser diodes?

Single-mode laser diodes produce a narrow Gaussian beam with only one transverse mode, supporting high spatial coherence and precision. In contrast, multimode laser diodes emit multiple transverse modes, resulting in a wider, less focused beam with higher output power but lower spatial quality.

What is the difference between single-mode and multimode fiber-coupled laser diodes?

Single-mode fiber supports only a single transverse mode with a small core, enabling long-distance, high-bandwidth transmission. Multimode fiber supports multiple light modes with a larger core, suitable for shorter distances. Single-mode laser diodes can couple to both fiber types, but multimode laser diodes only couple efficiently with multimode fiber.

What is the difference between optical linewidth, spectral bandwidth, modulation bandwidth and data rate?

Optical linewidth and spectral bandwidth both describe the light itself: linewidth is the width of a single-frequency laser line, usually given in MHz or kHz (our DFB lasers are typically below 1 MHz), while spectral bandwidth is the width of a broader spectrum, usually given in nm (for example 23 to 40 nm FWHM for our SLDs). Modulation bandwidth and data rate describe the electrical signal: modulation bandwidth, in GHz, is the highest frequency at which a laser or photodiode responds efficiently (up to 20 GHz for our fastest photodiodes), and data rate, in Gbps, is how many bits per second a digital link carries (for example our 2.5 and 10 Gbps lasers).

Superluminescent Diodes

What are superluminescent diodes (SLDs)?

Superluminescent diodes (SLDs) are broadband, incoherent light sources. Their emission spectrum is similar to LEDs, but they are more directional and can be efficiently coupled into optical fibers, making them ideal for fiber-optic sensing, imaging, and high-precision photonics applications.

What superluminescent diodes does LD4B offer?

LD4B offers fiber-coupled superluminescent diodes (SLDs) with wavelengths ranging from 670 nm to 1550 nm and optical powers of a few milliwatts.

Photodiodes

What are photodiodes?

Photodiodes are optoelectronic devices that convert light into electrical current. They are widely used in fiber-optic communication, sensing, and measurement systems for high-speed and precise optical detection.

What photodiodes does LD4B offer?

LD4B offers PIN photodiodes, avalanche photodiodes (APDs), and photodiodes with integrated transimpedance amplifiers (TIA). These photodiodes are designed for high-speed optical detection in microwave photonics, OTDR, digital communication, fiber-optic sensing and security applications.

How do I choose between a PIN photodiode and an avalanche photodiode (APD)?

Choose a PIN photodiode when the signal is strong enough or you need the highest bandwidth, linearity and simple low-voltage operation. Choose an APD when the signal is weak, for example in LIDAR or OTDR: its internal gain improves sensitivity at the cost of a high bias voltage.

Should I choose a photodiode alone or a receiver with an integrated TIA?

PIN photodiodes and APDs are intended for analog applications. PIN-TIA and APD-TIA modules include an automatic gain control unit and are intended for digital applications.

Which photodiodes are intended for analog signals, and which for digital communication?

Our PIN photodiodes (PD4B) and APDs (APD4B) give a current proportional to the optical power, so they suit analog applications such as RF-over-fiber, microwave photonics, power monitoring, LIDAR and OTDR, and they can also be used in digital receivers with your own amplifier. Our PIN-TIA (PINTIA4B) and APD-TIA (APDTIA4B) receivers are designed for digital communication: they have a built-in transimpedance amplifier with an automatic gain control unit.

Should I use a silicon, InGaAs or extended-InGaAs photodiode?

Choose by wavelength: silicon for visible and near-infrared light from 400 to 1000 nm, InGaAs for roughly 800 to 1700 nm, including the 1310 and 1550 nm telecom bands, and extended InGaAs for longer wavelengths up to about 2150 nm, such as 2 µm sources.

Fibers and connectors

Which optical fibers does LD4B couple light to?

LD4B specializes in coupling light to single-mode and polarization-maintaining fibers with core diameters from 3 to 10 µm. We also provide fiber coupling for multimode fibers with core sizes of 50 µm, 62.5 µm, and 105 µm if it is required for specific application.

Should I choose an APC or UPC connector, and can they be connected together?

Choose APC (angled polish, usually a green boot) for DFB lasers, SLDs and low back-reflection photodetectors: when mated to another APC connector in an adapter, back-reflection is below −60 dB, and in free space the reflected light is directed out of the fiber, but note that the output beam leaves at about 4° to the fiber axis. Choose UPC (straight polish, usually a black or blue boot) for FP lasers and most photodetectors: back-reflection is about −14 dB from the bare end face in free space and below −40 dB when mated to another UPC connector, and the beam leaves along the fiber axis. APC and UPC connectors must never be mated together, because this causes high loss and can damage the end faces.

When should I choose polarization-maintaining fiber instead of standard single-mode fiber?

Choose polarization-maintaining (PM) fiber when your system is sensitive to polarization, for example in interferometric or coherent sensing, with external modulators, or with other polarization-dependent components, because in standard single-mode (SM) fiber the polarization drifts with bending, vibration and temperature. If polarization doesn't matter, standard SM fiber is the simpler choice. Most of our single-mode lasers and SLDs are available with either fiber, but note that coupling efficiency into PM fiber is typically lower.

What is the difference between polarization extinction ratio (PER) and polarization-dependent loss (PDL)?

PER describes how well light stays linearly polarized: it is the ratio of the power in the intended polarization axis to the power in the orthogonal axis, so a higher PER (in dB) is better, and it is the key parameter for lasers and SLDs with polarization-maintaining fiber. PDL describes how much a component's loss or response changes with the input polarization: it is the difference between the maximum and minimum transmission over all polarization states, so a lower PDL (in dB) is better, and it matters mainly for photodiodes and passive components. For our photodiodes, refractive-index-matched (RM) coupling gives the lowest PDL.

Technology

What packages does LD4B offer?

LD4B offers coaxial packages (COAX, COAXB, COAXH and TH) and the 14-pin BTF package with thermal stabilization (TEC and thermistor). Lasers of the coaxTEC series also have a TEC and a thermistor, built into the standard COAXB package. Compact coaxial packages suit low-heat modules like photodiodes or laser diodes in low duty cycle pulse mode. For CW laser diodes or SLDs, coaxial packages with brackets allow heat sink attachment.

Which package should I choose?

Choose by heat load and mounting. COAX is the most compact and lightweight package and can be soldered directly into a PCB; without a heat sink it suits photodiodes, laser diodes in pulse mode with a small duty cycle, or CW operation at a small current, as long as the total released heat stays below 30 mW. COAXB and COAXH add a bracket for mounting to a heat sink, a device case or a board, and suit CW laser diodes and SLDs. TH fits the Thorlabs LDM9LP mount and CLD101xLP controller. When the wavelength and optical power must stay stable, choose a module with a TEC and a thermistor: a coaxTEC laser keeps the size of the coaxial package, and BTF is the industry-standard 14-pin butterfly case.

Does my module need a heat sink?

For long and reliable operation, every module should be attached to a heat sink, unless it is a COAX module releasing less than 30 mW of heat. The COAXB bracket has M2 threaded holes on the top and bottom sides and holes without thread or with M2.5 thread on the front side for panel mounting. A coaxTEC or BTF module always needs a heat sink, because both the laser diode and the TEC release heat. Remember to use thermal grease.

What are the thermistor and TEC parameters of the BTF package?

The thermistor is an NTC thermistor with R0 = 10 kOhm at 25 °C (T0 = 298 K) and B = 3600 K, so the absolute temperature follows from 1/T = 1/T0 + (1/B) ln(R/R0). We offer two TEC types: LP for laser diodes releasing up to 250–300 mW of heat (Imax = 0.7 A, Umax = 3.9 V, Qmax = 1.4 W, AC resistance 4.7 Ohm, dTmax = 72 K) and HP for larger heat loads (Imax = 1.4 A, Umax = 3.9 V, Qmax = 3.3 W, AC resistance 2.0 Ohm, dTmax = 69 K). The BTF application note shows the resistance curve and the measured cooling performance.

What are the TEC and thermistor parameters of the coaxTEC series?

The TEC of a coaxTEC laser has a maximum current of 1.2 A and a maximum voltage of 2.5 V. The thermistor is an NTC thermistor with R0 = 10 kOhm at 25 °C and B = 3900 K, so the absolute temperature follows from 1/T = 1/T0 + (1/B) ln(R/R0) with T0 = 298 K. A coaxTEC laser comes in the standard COAXB package, but with a different pin assignment, shown on the drawing in each datasheet.

Does LD4B offer controllers for laser diodes?

LD4B does not manufacture laser diode controllers directly but offers controllers from trusted partner companies. Customers can order these controllers together with LD4B fiber-coupled laser diodes for seamless compatibility and integration. Contact us for selection help.

Are there drawings and 3D models of LD4B modules available?

Yes, LD4B datasheet PDFs include active buttons with hyperlinks to detailed module drawings in PDF format and 3D models in STEP files, enabling easy access to mechanical and dimensional data for precise integration and design.

Which technologies does LD4B use?

At LD4B, we specialize in advanced fiber-lensing and active alignment techniques to achieve highly efficient fiber coupling of laser diodes, superluminescent diodes (SLDs), and photodiodes. Our proprietary methods ensure low-loss light delivery, excellent stability, and reliable performance in demanding optical systems.

What is special about LD4B’s laser diode pigtailing technology?

LD4B employs two specialized pigtailing technologies tailored for different application priorities. The first technology achieves higher coupling efficiency of 70–80 % with a typical thermal variation of about 0.5 dB in the telecom wavelength range. The second technology prioritizes exceptional stability of the coupling against temperature fluctuations, with a tracking error of 0.2–0.3 dB at the cost of lower coupling efficiency of 50–60 %. These options enable customers to choose fiber coupling solutions optimized either for maximum optical power or for superior thermal stability, depending on their system requirements.

What is special about LD4B’s photodiode pigtailing technology?

LD4B utilizes three distinct photodiode pigtailing technologies to optimize performance for different application needs. The R50 technology minimizes back-reflection, which is crucial for reducing the influence on other active components in a fiber link. The RM technology employs refractive index matching between the optical fiber and the photodiode to effectively eliminate Fresnel reflections at the fiber–air interface, thereby increasing responsivity and reducing PDL. This approach enhances overall detector sensitivity and signal quality, making it ideal for high-precision optical measurement systems. The LS technology allows for coupling from multimode optical fibers into a photodiode of smaller active area.

How does LD4B test the reliability of pigtailed modules?

LD4B focuses on the reliability of fiber alignment and packaging in our assemblies. We rigorously test our module designs following the Telcordia GR-468-CORE standard, a leading industry benchmark for optical component reliability. Our comprehensive testing includes mechanical shock and vibration, multiple thermal cycling, high and low temperature storage, thermal shocks, fiber integrity, and leak tests. During production every module passes 10 passive thermal cycles from −40 °C to +85 °C. These tests ensure our modules deliver stable, long-term performance in demanding industrial environments.

Under what conditions does LD4B assemble pigtailed modules?

LD4B assembles pigtailed modules in controlled clean environment within laminar flow hoods to maintain high standards of cleanliness and contamination control. Strict precautions against electrostatic discharge (ESD) are implemented throughout the process. Each assembled module undergoes thorough electrical and optical testing to ensure optimal performance before delivery.

How are LD4B modules packed?

Each LD4B module is carefully packed to ensure protection and reliability during shipping and handling. Modules are placed in vacuum-sealed, ESD-safe bags to prevent static discharge and contamination. They are further cushioned with ESD-safe bubble foil and securely enclosed in sturdy carton boxes to safeguard against mechanical damage. This packaging approach preserves the modules' integrity until they reach the customer.

Ordering

How can I place an order with LD4B?

What are the warranty terms?

LD4B standard warranty covers our modules for one year from the date of delivery or up to 5,000 operating hours, whichever comes first. The warranty protects against manufacturing defects and intrinsic material faults, provided the product has not been altered, incorrectly installed, misused, or repaired by the user.

What happens if a module fails?

In case of failure, the customer must notify LD4B in writing within one week of discovering the fault, providing detailed information. The faulty product must be returned before the warranty expires. LD4B will perform a failure analysis free of charge and will repair or replace any product that fails under warranty conditions. Shipping costs for returning products are not covered by the warranty. After the warranty period, failure root cause analysis is still available at no cost, but shipping expenses remain the responsibility of the customer.

Does LD4B provide test datasheets?

Yes, absolutely. Every LD4B module undergoes comprehensive electrical and optical testing, and a detailed test datasheet (TDS) is generated for each unit. The TDS is sent to customers via email, and a printed copy of the general datasheet is included in the shipment for reference. This ensures customers receive full transparency on their module’s performance and specifications.

Can LD4B customize the fiber, connector, fiber length or package?

Yes. Every module is built to order, so besides the standard options in the datasheet we can supply other fiber types, connectors, fiber lengths and packages. Many components are in stock, while others are ordered specifically for your project, so tell us your requirements and we'll confirm what's possible.
